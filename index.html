Current project

Beating the market with automation

Projects

I've always been drawn to sports betting as a numbers problem. Every price is a prediction, and if a model can estimate the odds better than the crowd, the gap is measurable. Prediction markets like Kalshi make that testable: prices update live, trade like a market, and can be automated through an API.

The question

Late in close NBA games, the market sets a price on whether a team clears a point total. Can a model that reads the live game state price that better than the market does?

Why automation

Late-game prices move every few seconds. A system has to watch the game, compute the odds, compare them with the price, and decide faster and more consistently than a person can.

What I built

A data pipeline, a calibrated machine-learning model, a backtest against real Kalshi prices, and a tracker that grades the bot on every new game.

Tracker · every game since Nov 11, 2026

Each morning, an automated job pulls the previous night's finished games, replays each 4th quarter against Kalshi's real prices, and records whether the bot would have bought, when, and how it turned out. The model and rule have been locked since Nov 11, so every game here is new to it.

How it works

From play-by-play to a bet

Kalshi lists contracts like “Houston over 113.5 points.” Each pays $1 if it hits and costs whatever the market charges, so an 80¢ price means the market thinks it is about 80% likely. The model makes its own estimate from the game state. When its number is higher than the price by enough to cover the fee, that is a bet.

STEP 1Collect

Play-by-play for 7,289 games from NBA and ESPN data, plus 12,810 Kalshi markets and 1M+ minute-level prices.

STEP 2Unify

Two data formats turned into one event table: score, clock, team fouls, timeouts, possession.

STEP 3Snapshot

Every 20 seconds of every 4th quarter, for both teams and several target scores. 2.7M rows.

STEP 4Predict

Gradient-boosted model (LightGBM) outputs the chance the team reaches the line.

STEP 5Decide

Edge = model chance − Kalshi ask − fee. Bet when the edge clears the threshold.

No peeking. Seasons are split by year, never randomly, because moments from the same game are near-duplicates. Team ratings only use games played before the one being predicted.

What I learned

The model isn't the bottleneck

Speed decides it

With the same model and the same bets, acting a minute late turned a profit into a loss. Many apparent edges were moments when the price hadn't caught up to a play yet. I'm now logging ESPN's feed delay against live Kalshi price moves to measure the real gap.

Markets are thin

A typical trading minute on team totals moved about 33 contracts. Sizing has to follow volume, which points toward combined game totals, where trading is roughly 3–20× heavier.

The game is drifting

4th-quarter scoring rose about 2 points per team in recent seasons, which made models trained on older data slightly too optimistic. Recent seasons need extra weight.

Simple beats clever

Team ratings and momentum features didn't improve the held-out score. Testing each feature group separately showed that before it could quietly add noise.

The obvious strategy loses

Buying the late 80–92¢ favorite without a model lost about a quarter of every dollar staked. Favorites at that price hit less often than the price implies.
